## Overview

Nykaa is one of India's largest beauty e-commerce platforms. Its Product Detail Page (PDP) is where shoppers decide whether to buy, yet on mobile the first screen showed **little more than a product image**. Brand, price, ratings and offers were all below the fold.

I redesigned the page so everything a shopper needs to decide is visible **before the first scroll**, on every phone and browser. I then extended the work into offers, pricing and social proof.

## The problem

Session recordings, heatmaps and an audit of the live page showed six pain points:

- **Nothing useful in the first scroll:** no brand, price or rating in view
- **The image took the whole screen**, and shoppers struggled to get back from the full-size view
- **Offers were hard to find**, even though price drives the purchase
- **Ratings were invisible** in a category where reviews decide the sale
- **Price and discount were buried** far below the image
- **The browser ate the fold:** Safari's toolbar and Chrome's address bar each hid a different part of the screen

---

## How I approached it

**1. Research.** Studied recordings, scroll depth and the device and browser split, then wrote six hypotheses about how people shop for beauty products.

**2. Explore, review, refine.** Worked through six rounds of options: layout directions, screen sizes, coupons, ratings and the price button. Each round ended with review feedback or a documented trade-off that shaped the next one.

**3. Ship in two phases.** Phase 1 brought identity, ratings and an offers entry point into the first fold. Phase 2 added a sticky price bar and brought shade choices into view.

**4. Scale it into a system.** Defined one set of pricing rules for member prices, flash sales and best price across all three platforms, and ran a social proof A/B test on app and web.

---

## Key decisions

| Decision | Why | What I gave up |
|---|---|---|
| A one-line **coupon strip** instead of a large coupon card | Shows savings exist and leads straight to them, using the coupons we already had | One extra tap to see the coupons themselves |
| An **image that resizes to the screen** | Brand and price stay visible on small and large phones alike | A smaller product image on small phones |
| **View similar and Wishlist on the image** | Freed a full row of space in the first fold | Smaller buttons, so I gave them generous tap areas |
| A **sticky price bar** with discount and original price | Price stays in view however far the shopper scrolls | A slightly taller bottom bar |
| **One pricing system** for all offers | Stopped multiple price banners competing on one page | A set of rules to maintain for each scenario |

## What I learned

- **Design for the real browser, not the artboard.** Testing in Safari and Chrome changed the layout more than any review.
- **Write the trade-off down.** Comparing options side by side made decisions faster and easier to explain.
- **Rules scale better than screens.** One pricing system replaced dozens of one-off mockups.
- **Agree on what failure looks like first.** Setting a fail criterion before the A/B test kept the team honest.
