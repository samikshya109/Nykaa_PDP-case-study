Case study · Nykaa · Product Detail Page

Making the first scroll do the selling

A redesign of Nykaa's Product Detail Page across iOS, Android and mobile web. Shoppers landed on a page where the product image filled the first screen and the price, rating and offers sat below it. I rebuilt the first fold so everything needed to decide is visible before the first scroll, then extended it into pricing, offers and social proof.

Overview

The project in four lines

  1. S

    Situation

    The PDP on mobile web carries as much traffic as the app homepage, yet most shoppers left the first screen having seen only a product image. Brand, price, rating and offers were all below the fold, and the layout behaved differently across Safari on iOS and Chrome on Android.

  2. T

    Task

    Bring brand, product name, price, discount, category and shade or size choices into the first fold on every device and browser, without losing the image-led browsing beauty shoppers rely on.

  3. A

    Action

    Audited recordings and heatmaps, wrote six hypotheses, explored six rounds of options with review feedback between each, then shipped Phase 1 and Phase 2. Extended the work to Privé early-access pricing and a social proof A/B test on app and web.

  4. R

    Result

    Every decision-making element now appears in the first fold, even on a 640 px tall screen. Add-to-bag, offer engagement and scroll depth all improved (sample figures above), and pricing widgets became one system across all three platforms.

What was the PDP actually doing?

Research objective

Improve the visibility of important information in the first fold of the PDP, covering every device and browser our shoppers use: Safari and Chrome on iOS, Chrome and other browsers on Android.

Two observations that shaped the work

User pain points

Six reasons shoppers didn't add to bag

  1. P1
    Nothing useful in the first scroll

    No brand, price or rating above the fold, so nothing drove an instant decision.

  2. P2
    The image takes the whole screen

    Most of the viewport went to one image, and shoppers who opened the large view struggled to get back.

  3. P3
    Offers are hard to find

    No way to explore coupons and offers from the top of the page, even though price drives the purchase.

  4. P4
    Ratings are invisible

    No rating or review signal in view, in a category where social validation decides the purchase.

  5. P5
    Price and discount are buried

    The most important purchase factor sat far below the image, with no discount or MRP context.

  6. P6
    Browser chrome eats the fold

    Safari's toolbar, Chrome's address bar and promo banners shrank the usable screen differently on each device.

Hypotheses

What I believed, and set out to test

  1. Shoppers arrive already knowing the product. Most land from social campaigns, so recognising the image matters more than discovering it.
  2. Beauty is recognised by image, then name. Brand and product name can sit directly below the image without losing context.
  3. Intent is either fixed or exploratory. Give fixed-intent shoppers enough to buy in the first scroll, and explorers a path through breadcrumbs and View similar.
  4. Price decides conversion. Shoppers actively look for discounts, coupons and offers.
  5. Shoppers want second opinions. They check detailed reviews and buyer photos before buying.
  6. Social proof builds trust. Showing how many people recently added a product will lift add-to-bag, especially for new visitors.

      Solutions by platform

      What shipped on each platform

      Each platform had its own constraints, so the same ideas landed differently. Tags like P3 point back to the pain point a change solves.

      Situation
      iOS shoppers browse in Safari, whose bottom toolbar takes about 110 pt, and in the Nykaa app. Both hid price and rating below the image.
      Task
      Fit decision-making information above Safari's toolbar, follow iOS patterns for sheets and keyboards, and carry savings and trust signals into the app.
      Action
      Moved the price bar above the toolbar, used bottom sheets for notify-me and best price, and added social proof and a Privé callout in the app.
      Result
      Sample +16% add-to-bag on iOS Safari and 2.6× taps on the coupon strip.

      Price bar that clears Safari's toolbar

      P1P5P6

      Price, discount, MRP and Add to Bag sit in a sticky bar directly above Safari's toolbar. Share moved into the header so it stays one row.

      Trade-off: the bar costs about 64 pt of image height, accepted because price was the most-searched detail.

      Native sheets and keyboard

      P2

      Out-of-stock shoppers get a bottom sheet with one email field. With the keyboard open, the field and the action stay visible above it.

      Why: a full-page form would take shoppers away from the product they wanted.

      Social proof on App PDP

      P4

      A soft gradient strip above Add to bag shows how many people added the product in the last 7 days.

      Why: next to the CTA, the proof appears at the moment of decision.

      Situation
      About four in five web sessions come from Android, mostly Chrome with its top address bar and a bottom navigation bar, on a wide range of screen heights.
      Task
      Make the first fold hold up from 640 px to tall screens, and keep app and web consistent for the same product.
      Action
      Shipped Phase 1 and 2 on Chrome, sized the image to the screen, added the sticky price CTA, and designed the Hybrid PDP card and cart price breakup.
      Result
      Sample +13% add-to-bag on Android and 18% more shoppers reaching reviews.

      The full page, reordered

      P1P3P4

      Shades, coupons and offers follow the product identity. Details, ratings and buyer photos come next, and the price bar stays pinned throughout.

      Why: fixed-intent shoppers get what they need early; explorers still reach reviews in one scroll.

      Hybrid PDP in the app

      P3P4

      The Hybrid PDP card carries rating, price, size and social proof, with Collect Coupons and Offers as expandable rows.

      Trade-off: rows collapsed by default keep the card short, at the cost of one tap to see coupons.

      Price breakup in the bag

      P5

      An info icon opens a breakup that shows the early-access offer replacing the smaller MRP discount.

      Why: explaining the final price builds trust at checkout.

      Situation
      Mweb runs in Safari and Chrome on iOS and Chrome and other browsers on Android, each with different chrome, and catches all campaign traffic.
      Task
      Design one PDP that adapts to every browser and screen height, with rules the web team can build once.
      Action
      Covered four browsers, defined adaptive image heights, chose the coupon strip, wrote pricing widget rules and ran the social proof test.
      Result
      Sample One layout across four browsers and +3.1% conversion in the social proof test group.

      One layout, four browsers

      P6

      The same PDP was checked in Safari iOS, Chrome iOS, Chrome Android and Samsung Internet, at large and small heights.

      Why: browser chrome differs by up to 300 px, so a layout tuned to one browser breaks in another.

      Social proof A/B test

      P4

      Traffic split into a control and variants by social proof type. The same strip was designed for every surface.

      Fail criterion, set up front: no improvement in trust, cart additions, conversion or order value.

      Learnings

      What I'd keep doing

      • Design for the browser, not the frame. A 360 × 800 artboard hides how much Safari and Chrome take away. Testing in real browser chrome changed the layout more than any review.
      • Write down the trade-off. Pros and cons for each coupon option made the decision quick and kept coupons consistent across the app.
      • Rules beat screens. The pricing matrix replaced dozens of one-off mocks and let three platforms ship the same behaviour.
      • Agree on failure first. Writing the fail criterion before the social proof test kept the team honest about what counted as a win.
